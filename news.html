County budget / jail and law-enforcement funding · Ocala Gazette published November 8, 2026

County says no budget cuts have been decided despite $87.7 million property-tax projection

New post-election statement: In reporting published November 8, the Ocala Gazette quoted Marion County Public Relations Manager Stacie Causey responding to questions sent to commissioners about Amendment 3. She said the county continues evaluating the financial implications, has made no final decisions on future allocations or service changes, and has established no timeline for those decisions.

County estimates, not adopted cuts: The Gazette reports that the county's existing Amendment 3 materials project reduced property-tax receipts of $49.6 million in the first affected fiscal year and $38.1 million in the following year (approximately $87.7 million combined). The same reporting describes roughly $42 million in potential law-enforcement and detention impacts across the two years under the county's scenarios. These countywide estimates are a different scope from Sheriff Woods' previously reported $61 million departmental warning; the figures should not be added together or reported as approved reductions.

Not established: No jail treatment service closure, staffing cut, final millage rate or spending reduction is proved by a forecast. The property-tax measure passed, but its exact effects depend on future budgets and implementation.

Accountability questions: Will county and Sheriff's Office leaders release reconciled fund-by-fund scenarios, identify which detention healthcare obligations are protected, and publish proposed service reductions before budget votes?

Sources: Ocala Gazette, Andy Fillmore (published Nov. 8, 2026; includes county's response); Marion County's Property Tax Amendment 3 materials (pre-election estimates, linked by the Gazette; county page not independently retrievable during this review).

County Commission / land-use rules · Official agenda file created October 29 · Hearing November 9, 2026, 9 a.m.

Final public hearing scheduled on disputed off-site advertising sign amendments

Confirmed in the official agenda: Marion County commissioners scheduled their second and final public hearing for November 9 at 9 a.m. at the McPherson Governmental Campus Auditorium. Official file 2026-24918 concerns a privately initiated amendment to off-site sign setbacks and proximity to occupied homes.

Two distinct changes: The applicant proposes allowing sign faces to extend into 15-foot side setbacks between commercial or industrial parcels, with survey and easement conditions. A second change would extend existing 300-foot residential separation protections to homes on property not zoned residential. County staff estimates the first change could add about 63 candidate sign locations, while the second could remove about 232. Staff opposes the setback exception but supports the added home protection, and recommends adopting only the second change.

Not established: The posted November 9 record remains “Agenda Ready,” with no final action reported at this morning's review. No ordinance adoption or denial is claimed, and the estimates are not permits or constructed signs.

Accountability questions: How will commissioners balance commercial signage, existing homes, road aesthetics, and staff's comprehensive-plan concerns? Will the final vote and ordinance language be published promptly?

Official sources: File 2026-24918 (created Oct. 29; reviewed Nov. 9); November 9 hearing agenda.

Sheriff's Office / contracted child welfare · WCJB Nov. 5; WKMG News 6 Nov. 6

Case manager accused of failing to report suspected foster-care abuse

Confirmed and reported: Marion County Sheriff's Office deputies arrested Cristina Feliz, 35, on November 3. News reports identify her as a case manager employed by Kids Central Inc., a contractor providing child-welfare services for the Florida Department of Children and Families (DCF). She had worked with surviving siblings of Paisley Brown, the child who died in February. The arrest and criminal accusation are confirmed by the reporting, not a finding of guilt.

Allegation: According to the arrest report as described by WCJB and WKMG, Feliz received information about possible abuse by a foster caregiver but did not make the required abuse-hotline report, reportedly treating it as third-party information. The foster caregiver was arrested on a separate child-abuse allegation in September. Both criminal matters require their own evidence and judicial determination. Do not infer that the surviving children or anyone else has been adjudicated guilty of an offense.

Agency response and public standard: In statements reported November 6, Kids Central said it was cooperating with law enforcement; DCF said it would work with investigators and the contractor. Florida Statutes § 39.201 requires immediate reporting when a person knows or has reasonable cause to suspect child abuse. Reporting the arrest or summarizing the statute does not establish that the statutory elements have been proved in court.

Accountability questions: Did contractor supervision, mandatory-reporter training, and escalation procedures work as intended? Will DCF and Kids Central disclose any substantiated corrective action while protecting the children's privacy? WKMG reports a December 8 court date; this is a scheduled future step, not a disposition.

Sources: WCJB (published Nov. 5, 2026); WKMG News 6 (published Nov. 6, 2026; includes DCF and contractor statements); 2026 Florida Statutes § 39.201 (official law).

Sheriff's Office / emergency management · Ocala Gazette investigation published November 4, 2026

Who is directing Marion County emergency management after September retirement?

Documented and attributed: The Ocala Gazette reported that Preston Bowlin retired as emergency management director effective September 1, but on November 4 County Administrator Mounir Bouyounes said Sheriff Billy Woods had not informed county leaders who would replace him. The Florida Division of Emergency Management reportedly confirmed Bowlin remained its director of record; Ocala city officials also had not been told of a successor. School officials were working with Deputy Director Erin Miller, who had not been publicly named as the new director. The Sheriff's Office had not answered the newspaper's questions or fulfilled its October 26 records request at publication.

Official legal standard: Florida Statutes § 252.38(1)(b) requires a county emergency-management director, provides for appointment by the county commission or county chief administrative officer, and requires prompt notification to the state. A constitutional officer or employee may serve following prior state notification. The sheriff administers Marion County's emergency management function under its county arrangement; the Gazette reports a $1,300,372 county general-fund allocation for fiscal year 2026–27.

What remains unknown: The reporting does not prove the job is unfilled, emergency response is impaired, or anyone violated the law. It establishes a public documentation and communication gap as of November 4, not a legal finding.

Accountability question: Who is currently appointed or acting as director, what is the effective appointment date, and when were the commission, municipalities, school district and state formally notified?

Sources: Ocala Gazette, Jennifer Hunt (published Nov. 4, 2026); 2026 Florida Statutes § 252.38 (official law, checked Nov. 6).

Elected officials / voting access · Ocala Gazette analysis published November 3, 2026

Write-in candidacies restricted participation in two county-commission primaries

Reported records: An Ocala Gazette review of the August 18 election says filings by write-in candidates kept the District 2 and District 4 Republican commission primaries closed to 127,131 voters registered outside the Republican Party. Florida's universal-primary rule would otherwise have allowed all registered voters to participate in a party's primary if the winner faced no general-election opponent. The Gazette reports that one write-in candidate later withdrew and that District 4's Jeff Bairstow won his four-candidate primary with 39.02% of votes cast.

Important distinction: The registration and candidacy effect is a reported consequence of election rules, not evidence that an identified candidate acted unlawfully or that an election result was fraudulent. This newly examined ballot-access issue is separate from the county's already published November 3 election-night totals.

Accountability question: Should the Legislature revisit how write-in filings affect universal-primary access, and will future county candidate filings and financial disclosures be readily searchable?

Sources: Ocala Gazette, Jennifer Hunt (published Nov. 3, 2026); Marion County Supervisor of Elections, official election resources (checked Nov. 6).

Sheriff's Office and jail funding · WCJB report published November 4, 2026 · Election November 3

No specific jail program termination, body-camera reduction or deputy layoff has been authorized in the records reviewed. Accountability question: Will the Sheriff's Office and county publish a program-by-program fiscal analysis, protect medically necessary jail care and treatment, disclose any proposed body-camera changes, and hold public hearings before cuts are adopted?

County funding · County statement reported October 26, 2026 · November 3 ballot

County warns Amendment 3 could affect libraries and public safety funding

Confirmed proposal: Florida's Amendment 3, on the November 3 ballot, would increase the homestead exemption for non-school property taxes to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028 if approved by at least 60% of voters. This would lower qualifying homeowners' non-school property taxes. Public schools' property-tax levies would remain outside the expanded exemption.

County projections, not approved cuts: In an October 26 report, 352today quoted Marion County commissioners' statement estimating roughly $90 million less in county property-tax revenue across two fiscal years compared with an estimated $317 million in collections over the same period. The county projected approximately $35 million less for its General Fund over that period and said its library system could face a roughly $6 million reduction in 2027, approximately half its current budget, without offsetting tax-rate or fee changes. The county cautioned that public safety and other services could also be affected.

What is not established: The amendment has not been adopted by voters. These figures are county estimates under stated assumptions; no library closure, employee layoff, Sheriff's Office budget reduction, or final allocation has been approved on the basis of this forecast. The county said it has made no final service decisions.

Different positions: Supporters emphasize property-tax relief for eligible homeowners and potential spending restraint. Critics warn that less local revenue could strain services. The measure does not guarantee a minimum level of spending on law enforcement, fire response, or other services.

Oversight questions: Will commissioners publish their underlying forecast, options for mitigating reductions, and potential effects on jail healthcare, deputies, emergency response, libraries, and services used by residents?

Sources: 352today, Jamie Berube (published Oct. 26, 2026, including the Marion County government statement); City of Palm Coast official explanation of proposed Amendment 3 (undated reference page); PolitiFact analysis of the public-safety provisions (Oct. 13, 2026).
