Jail healthcare · Agreement effective October 1 · Investigation published October 14, 2026

New contractor takes over jail healthcare under three-year agreement

Document-based reporting: The Ocala Gazette, citing signed agreements, vendor scoring sheets and negotiation drafts, reports that NaphCare Florida LLC replaced Heart of Florida Health Center to provide medical, dental, mental health and pharmacy services at the Marion County Jail. The agreement runs from Oct. 1, 2026, to Sept. 30, 2029, with a reported first-year price of $17,168,399. NaphCare ranked third in the initial scoring, behind MEDIKO and Heart of Florida, but received the award after evaluation and negotiations.

Contractual requirements, not proven outcomes: As described in the records reviewed by the Gazette, the agreement adds deductions for unfilled staffing hours, specified sick-call response timelines, a prescription voucher for some released inmates and medical-record migration requirements. Whether those terms are met must be judged against subsequent compliance reports.

Allegations distinguished: Prior complaints about Heart of Florida's staffing and treatment, including those raised by a former nurse, remain allegations and disputed claims; the award to NaphCare alone does not prove misconduct or improved care.

Oversight question: Will the Sheriff's Office disclose monthly staffing compliance, response-time performance, medical grievances and withheld payments under the new contract?

Sources: Ocala Gazette investigation (Oct. 14, 2026); Sheriff's Office purchasing division (official agency page; undated); RFQ 26/1 public bid notice (May 11, 2026).

County commissioners · Vote October 20 · Recap published October 21, 2026

49th Street Gateway development approved; Sheriff's Office projected staffing impact

Official proposal and reported decision: County agenda file 2026-24503 documents a roughly 105-acre mixed-use proposal at Northwest 44th Avenue and Northwest 49th Street, with up to 535 homes, a 500-room hotel, retail and offices. Only In Ocala reports the County Commission approved the land-use change and planned-unit-development rezoning 5–0 on Oct. 20; the official file still lists the proposal's agenda status rather than a final action. A September 29 report cited a Sheriff's Office project comment estimating one additional deputy and approximately $230,802 in first-year costs associated with the proposed population increase.

Distinction: The deputy estimate is a planning projection, not a documented appropriation or new hire. Final buildout and public-safety costs remain subject to later implementation and oversight.

Oversight question: How will increased patrol, traffic, emergency and infrastructure costs be tracked as development proceeds?

Sources: County official agenda, item 2026-24503 (Oct. 20, 2026); Only In Ocala recap (Oct. 21, 2026); 352today planning report (Sept. 29, 2026).

Ocala elected officials · Vote October 20 · City announcement October 23, 2026

Ocala eliminates city business tax receipts; some refunds expected

Official confirmation: The City of Ocala announced it adopted Ordinance 2027-1, ending the municipal business tax receipt requirement and related fees. Businesses that paid for the current renewal period may qualify for refunds; city staff said processing would begin by Jan. 14, 2027. Other applicable state and local licensing, zoning, permitting and building rules remain in force. A local review of the city action record reports the Oct. 20 vote was 5–0.

Oversight question: How many businesses receive refunds, and how will the city account for foregone tax revenue and reduced program costs?

Sources: City of Ocala announcement (Oct. 23, 2026; redistributed Oct. 24); Oct. 20 City Council vote recap (published Oct. 21, 2026).

No newer substantiated finding of jail staff misconduct, medical negligence or in-custody deaths was identified in this October 26 review. Existing reports and lawsuits must not be mistaken for new findings.

County Commission · Decision October 20 · Report published October 21, 2026

Commissioners reject proposed settlement in food-waste composting dispute

Confirmed record: Marion County's official October 20 agenda included a mediated agreement concerning a rejected special-use permit for food-waste processing at 8510 and 8548 NW Gainesville Road. The proposal would have led to another hearing, not immediately authorized the operation.

Reported result: 352today reported on October 21 that commissioners unanimously rejected the agreement. Neighbors raised concerns over odors, groundwater and nearby Fessenden Elementary School; those concerns are public testimony, not findings that contamination occurred. The applicant retains legal avenues to contest the decision. The county's final action summary was not available at review time.

Oversight question: What independent environmental evidence and future hearing records will be disclosed as the dispute proceeds?

Sources: 352today, Jamie Berube (Oct. 21, 2026); Official county item 2026-24718 (Oct. 20 agenda).

Ocala elected officials · Decision October 20 · Report published October 23, 2026

City manager reappointed as council debates financial oversight

Recorded and reported: The Ocala City Council's October 20 meeting included the reappointment of City Manager Peter Lee and approval of a further $190,936 for four fire-rescue vehicles. 352today reported that Lee's reappointment passed, with Councilman Jim Hilty casting the sole dissenting vote.

Disputed interpretations: Hilty criticized communication and oversight, citing the late renewal of a natural-gas franchise and the fire-vehicle purchasing issue. City Attorney William Sexton said his office was chiefly responsible for the franchise renewal delay and that both parties continued honoring the prior agreement. A fleet manager attributed the missed prepayment discount to staff miscommunication. Official staff materials calculate about $135,063 in interest and $56,000 in additional vendor discounts to offset the $190,936 added invoice amount. These competing explanations do not by themselves establish misconduct by Lee. Final meeting minutes were not available at review time.

Oversight question: Will the city publish reconciliation of the interest, promised discounts, and procurement-controls changes?

Sources: 352today, Jamie Berube (Oct. 23, 2026); Ocala City Council official Oct. 20 meeting record; Official fire-apparatus expenditure item 2026-2067.

Sheriff and public-safety spending · Official agenda October 20, 2026

County records flag public-safety budget amendments for follow-up

Confirmed in official agenda, not confirmed as approved: Marion County's October 20 meeting listed a $231,355 infrastructure-surtax budget amendment labeled “Infrastructure Tax Sheriff Regular” (item 5.1.12) and a $673,465 General Fund budget amendment for Public Safety Communications (item 5.1.8). Their purposes and final disposition require review of the supporting documents and finalized county action summary. Neither entry alone indicates waste or wrongdoing.

Oversight question: What projects or equipment do these amounts fund, and were the amendments adopted as proposed?

Source: Marion County official Oct. 20, 2026 agenda (items 5.1.8 and 5.1.12; action summary draft at Oct. 25 review).

County Commission · October 20, 2026

Commission reportedly approves six opioid-settlement grants totaling up to $764,868.94

Confirmed in official records: The county's October 20 agenda lists six proposed awards to recovery housing, a nurse home-visiting program, a recovery support center, and the Fifth Judicial Circuit State Attorney and Public Defender offices. Two of the proposals would provide up to $52,250 each for the prosecutor and public defender, including administrative costs.

Reported outcome: Only In Ocala reported on October 21 that commissioners approved all six items 5–0 on the consent agenda. The official meeting portal displayed a draft action summary when checked October 24; the final minutes were not yet available for independent verification.

Accountability question: What outcomes and spending records will demonstrate whether this funding improves access to treatment and diversion from incarceration?

Sources: Marion County Commission agenda (Oct. 20, 2026; items 7.2.2–7.2.7); Only In Ocala meeting recap (published Oct. 21, 2026).

Sheriff's Office · October 22, 2026

Deputies investigate fatal shooting; firearm recovered near schools

Reported facts: WKMG News 6 reported that two gunshot victims arrived at hospitals and one died. Sheriff's deputies were investigating a vehicle crash near Liberty Middle School and a firearm discovered near neighboring Hammett Bowen Jr. Elementary School. Investigators had not publicly identified the original shooting location as of the October 22 update.

Official position as reported: Deputies described the case as apparently isolated and said they did not believe there was a danger to the schools or public. This is an ongoing criminal investigation, not an allegation of misconduct by deputies.

Source: WKMG News 6 (published and updated Oct. 22, 2026).

County Commission · October 7, 2026 report

County reduces jail-cost lien burden in hardship case

Reported government action: Ocala-News.com reported that commissioners waived $701.75 in accrued interest on civil restitution liens connected with jail costs and allowed the remaining $1,550 principal to be repaid by promissory note. The person seeking relief said the lien had blocked an application for a grant to restore running water.

Public-policy issue: The action shows commissioners may consider individualized relief. It does not establish that similar relief is automatic. A broader review could examine how incarceration liens affect housing, employment and access to county assistance.

Sources: Ocala-News.com (published Oct. 7, 2026; Sept. 15 commission action); Clerk of Court commission-records search.

Jail oversight · Background development, August 12, 2026

Former jail medical liaison's whistleblower suit settled for $60,000

Reported settlement: The Ocala Gazette reported that former medical liaison Mary Tolbert Coy and her attorney were to receive a combined $60,000 in a settlement of Coy's retaliation lawsuit against Sheriff Billy Woods. The agreement includes no admission of wrongdoing.

Allegations, not findings: Coy had alleged she was fired after raising concerns about medical care and compliance at the jail. Woods disputed retaliation and maintained that the termination had lawful business reasons. The settlement does not establish whether the underlying medical-care allegations were true.

Open oversight question: What independent monitoring and publicly reportable metrics now apply to the jail's medical-services contract?

Sources: Ocala Gazette (published Aug. 12, 2026); Sheriff's Office official description of jail oversight responsibilities (undated).
